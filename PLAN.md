## Known risks

- **Background session lifetime (S1)**: in the simulator, a killed app was relaunched in the background and re-created both sessions without diagnostics. A user force-quit on a device is still untested (T4/T5). Running the control's intent was followed by an app relaunch in the simulator (2 of 2) for an unknown reason; not to be relied on until seen on a device.
- **Paused energy (S1)**: strategy A keeps location running while paused, so paused energy ≈ recording energy until measured (T7).
- **Spike code in the app**: resolved in VS1. S1 and S2 pages are only reachable from the Debug "Developer" menu; `S1RecordingControl` is out of the widget bundle (S1 device tests need an older build, see Next).
- **GCJ-02 detection (S2)**: MapKit does not report the base map's coordinate system (D17). From Japan the mainland tiles are WGS-84, so whether to convert depends on which map Apple serves, and no public signal for that is known yet. If none is found, rule 3 (no user setting) is at risk.
- **Border jump (S2)**: per-point conversion makes a ~500 m jump where a track crosses into HK or Macau. Rendering has to decide how to draw it.
- **OSM attribution (S2)**: `china-mainland.bin` contains ODbL data; "© OpenStreetMap contributors" must appear in the app before release (`docs/THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`).
- **Battery vs. completeness (S3)**: all-day recording under 3 % is the hardest quantitative target. Not shown impossible; levers and the rules they touch are in S3-result §8.
- **Strategy C wake-up (S3)**: no `CLMonitor` event at all in the simulator, foreground or background (S3-result §4.2). T-C0 on a device decides whether C is viable; restarting updates from that wake-up in the background (T-C1) is a second unknown.
- **Replayed fixes (S3)**: handled in VS1. Fixes older than the process launch are saved with the `replayed` flag, never decide the state, and are not drawn on Today.
- **Two location apps on the device (S3)**: Trace Notes now records by itself after onboarding. TN S3 is a separate app with its own engine; running both on one device breaks "one engine at a time" and mixes up the battery numbers.
- **Launch classification (VS1)**: iOS 26 deprecates `launchOptions[.location]`. `launch` vs. `backgroundRelaunch` is decided by whether the connected scene is in the foreground at the first fix (a simulator probe showed UIKit connects a scene on both kinds of launch). If a device ever relaunches without connecting a scene, no launch event is written for that process; `terminatedDetected` is unaffected.
- **Stationary + killed (VS1)**: while stationary the 25 m distance filter delivers no fixes, so after a kill the system only relaunches the app on movement (visit / significant change). In the simulator a killed stationary app stayed down; leaving the place relaunches it on a device (to check in device check 3).
- **Write window on a kill (VS1)**: points are written one by one through a single async writer; a point received in the last moment before a kill can be lost.
- **Processing only in the foreground (VS2)**: segments are derived while the app is on screen. Nothing else reads them yet; a future widget or notification would need background processing.
- **Stay start is approximate (VS2)**: the dwell anchor is checked against the newest point only, so a stay can begin up to ~30 s before the real arrival (simulator: 12:13:12 for an arrival at 12:13:43).
- **Visits carry no coordinates (VS2)**: VS1 stores only visit times, so a visit-only stay takes its centre from nearby points.
- **Indoor drift beyond 50 m (VS2)**: dwell needs usable points within 50 m for 5 minutes. Indoor drift wider than that is caught by the visit arrival or the silent-in-place rule; to check in device check 2.
- **Legacy values on a new density (stabilizer)**: the 30 values were tuned on the legacy app's 10 m delivery; the 10 m thinning reproduces that, but the legacy recorder used `CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates`, not this app's `CLLocationManager` settings. Device checks decide whether any value needs tuning.
- **One-point spikes need a clear return (stabilizer)**: the legacy rule removes a single jump only when the track comes back within 15 % of the jump's length; a 90 m sideways jump during a 14 m step is kept. Multi-point excursions are handled by the excursion detector.
- **Open move on the main thread (stabilizer)**: the map stabilizes the open move on every update; a multi-hour open drive (≈ 10 000 points) costs ~0.2 s per update in Debug. Moving the open move into the processor's cache is the fix if device check 5 stutters.
- **Whole-day redraw (VS3)**: every change of the open move (about once a second while moving, app open) rebuilds both day overlays. Fine for a few thousand cached points in the simulator; a long day on an older phone may stutter (device check 6). A split into a static overlay for completed moves and a small one for the open move is the fix if needed.
- **Level switch on zoom (VS3)**: crossing a level boundary replaces the overlays; MapKit may show one frame without the track. Not visible in the simulator.
- **Corner test uses the cached level's points (VS3)**: at coarse levels the legs are long, so more turns qualify as corners and are drawn straight; looks angular at city scale for winding roads.
- **Legacy stabilizer at all-day scale**: it holds the whole session in memory and does several O(n) copies or scans per sample (see LEGACY_INVENTORY §4.2). Its session `finish()` has no direct equivalent under D1.
- **City driving without Motion & Fitness (VS4)**: by speed alone, driving below 30 km/h is cycling (seen in the simulator for the city part of the Umeda → KIX drive). With the sensor allowed it is car; with it denied the user corrects it.
- **Car vs. train (VS4)**: both are "automotive" to the sensor; every train ride below 200 km/h is shown as a car until the user changes it (D36 ④). The share of corrections is not counted yet (PRODUCT F4 "本地统计用户修改率").
- **Simulator has no motion data (VS4)**: only the speed path was exercised end to end; the sensor path is covered by unit tests with synthetic readings, and the recorded payload format by one test.
- **Picker on the open move (VS4)**: the open move is rewritten on every run, so its row ID changes; a pick saves the override first and the next run applies it (seen in the simulator within the same second). If a move splits later, the pick goes to the piece covering most of the picked time range.
- **Gap between 7 and 10 km/h (VS4)**: the confirmed bands left 7–10 km/h open without a running reading; it is treated as cycling (D36 ②).
- **Neighbouring shop (VS5)**: the nearest point of interest to the stay's centre can be the shop next door when GPS drifts indoors; renaming fixes it for every later visit.
- **One place per 50 m (VS5)**: two real places closer than 50 m (two shops in one building) share one place and one name; a stay whose centre lands 50 m from an existing place makes a new one.
- **Name language (VS5)**: names come in the device language at lookup time (zh-Hans gives «全家便利商店…») and are not re-fetched when the language changes.
- **Lookups only in the foreground (VS5)**: like segment processing; a place stays unnamed until the app is opened.
- **Mainland China (VS5)**: lookups send WGS-84 centres; whether Apple's POI search in the mainland expects GCJ-02 is unknown (S2).
- **Subway needs motion readings during the ride (VS6)**: readings are only recorded while the app runs. If iOS suspends or kills the app underground, only the readings around the gap count (riding before and after); walking out of the station afterwards defeats that, so the subway may show as "not running". Fetching the missed readings from Core Motion's history on relaunch would fix it (a recording change, not done).
- **Subway rule is straight-line (VS6)**: 15–100 km/h between entry and exit. A ride with a long wait on the platform, or a winding line, can fall under 15 km/h; a very short hop under 1 km is never a subway. Not tuned on real rides.
- **Simulator has no motion data (VS6)**: the subway rule is covered by unit tests only; the simulator check exercised the "not running" cause, the dashed links and the gap menu.
- **Time zone labels not seen yet (VS7)**: the simulator data is all in one zone; the labels are only exercised by a day recorded in two zones (device check 5). Short names come from the system and depend on the language (some zones show as "GMT+9").
- **"Today" is the phone's current zone (VS7)**: on a trip, today follows the phone's zone at that moment, while each day's points file under the zone they were recorded in; right after a flight east, "today" can be a date with no points yet.
- **Recorded-days query on every segment write (VS7)**: the calendar's day list is re-read whenever a segment changes (about once a second while moving with the app open). One DISTINCT over the day index; fine for a year in the simulator data, not measured at several years.
- **Toolchain**: the machine has Xcode 27.0 (iOS 27 SDK) with an iOS 26.5 simulator runtime. The deployment target is 26.0.
- **Monochrome grey layer (D38, D40)**: relies on the `saturationBlendMode` compositing filter, which Apple does not document for iOS, and on MapKit's private view order (annotation container inside the map content view); either may change in a later iOS. A changed hierarchy is logged and leaves the map in colour.
- **Annotation-anchored track (D39, D40)**: in step with pans and held pinches in the simulator; held pinches on the device not yet confirmed. It relies on MapKit setting each anchor view's layer position when it applies a camera and moving both anchors in the same pass (observed, not documented). If MapKit rounds annotation positions to pixels on a device, the 0.3 × screen separation keeps the scale error under about 0.5 %. The whole day's path is re-transformed on each zoom frame (cost grows with the day's points at the shown level); pans need no redraw. Map pitch is off.
- **Relaunch after a kill (D41)**: rests on the live-updates subscription, an observed simulator behaviour (5 of 6 kills, one unexplained miss), not a documented promise; `simctl terminate` is not the same as a memory kill on a device. The subscription also runs the GPS at its own default settings next to the sampling strategy, which may cost battery while moving (it is expected to pause while stationary); not measured. If either fails on the device, the choice is back to the blue pill.
- **Way-out backfill (D38)**: only the fixes within one heartbeat interval before leaving are kept in memory; if the app was killed while stationary, the way out starts at the first fix after relaunch.
- **Overview path size (VS7.5)**: zoomed into a city, every day's track at a fine level goes into one path, rebuilt on each read and re-stroked on each zoom step. Fine for one day of simulator data; a year of commutes in one city is untested.
- **Overview table scan (VS7.5)**: `track_bounds` has no index (D44); each read scans the whole table. Fine for thousands of moves; add an index or R*Tree if it shows.
- **Walking exit (D51)**: 2 minutes of "moving" readings plus one point beyond 50 m + accuracy ends a stay. A phone carried around a large indoor place (office, mall) with drift beyond that may now split a stay; watch for short spurious moves.
- **Ways in and out of stays (D52)**: drawn from raw thinned points, not stabilized, so GPS jitter on a slow way out can show as a small kink. The way into the newest stay is computed when the move completes, against that stay's centre and spread at the time; replaying later may draw it slightly differently. Two places about 50 m apart can still be one stay with one marker between them.
- **GPX import rules for other apps' files (VS8, D53 ⑤)**: a file without Trace Notes' time zone extension is placed in the phone's time zone at import time, so a trip abroad imported at home lands on home-zone days; points without accuracy are stored as 0 (exact).
- **GPX import recompute (VS8)**: an import drops every segment from its earliest point on and the processor recomputes all later days in one run, holding their points in memory, like a pipeline version change; a large import far in the past is slow once. Overlap is decided when points are processed: a recording that arrives after an import of the same minutes (only possible when importing a file that covers the last few minutes) does not hide imported points already processed.
- **Fog of World import memory and time (VS8.5)**: the whole archive and every block are held in memory while thinning (186 MB peak, 1.8 s on a Mac for 87,000 blocks, optimized). Larger explored worlds grow with it; an iPhone may take several times longer. Debug builds are about 20× slower.
- **Fog of World distance (VS8.5, D54 ⑤)**: an estimate. Flights and other long straight lines in Fog of World count in full; the overlap test (same or neighbouring ~50 m cell as one of Trace Notes' completed moves) is recomputed from all moves' finest render cache whenever a move completes, which grows with years of records.
- **Fog of World drawing (VS8.5)**: the viewport's cells are thinned and smoothed on every read (off the main thread); a dense city at a coarse grid may take a moment in Debug builds. Very dense explored areas draw as many short lines; trails a cell apart merge into one thicker line; an area filled in solid thins to a branching skeleton.
- **GPX export size (VS8)**: counting and writing load all points of the range into memory; All Records over years of data may take a while and a lot of memory.
- **Collapsed sheet height (D49)**: the 104 pt floor rests on iOS 26's sheet behaviour measured in the simulator (floating card below about 102 pt). A later iOS may move that threshold; only the iPhone 17 Pro simulator was measured.
- **Paused state (VS7.5 → VS9)**: the status button and card show problems only; "Paused" must be visible there too (ARCHITECTURE §7) and needs a decision on card or orange icon when VS9 adds it.
- **Delete during a long write (VS10)**: the delete waits for the database writer; while a Fog of World import or a long processing run holds it, the "Deleting…" row stays until it ends. VACUUM on a large database can take seconds on an older phone.
- **Diagnostic report size (VS10)**: 30 days of recorder events and MetricKit JSON are shown in full; a noisy month may make a long report. Not measured on a device.
- **OSLogStore scope (VS10)**: only the current process's log is readable, so warnings and errors from before the last launch are not in the report.
- **Battery samples (D56)**: the whole phone's level, so screen use and other apps show in it; iOS may report the level in coarse steps, and in the background a sample is only taken when the app runs (a notification or a location delivery), so a still hour with no delivery shows its drop at the next sample.
